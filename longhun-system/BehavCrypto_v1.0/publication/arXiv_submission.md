## arXiv Abstract（≤1920 字符 · 提交前请再压缩计数）

```
We introduce Behavioral Cryptography, a multi-factor provenance framework
for authenticating human-AI collaborative content. Rather than asking only
whether content was generated by AI, the framework asks: who originated
this content, through which rules and personas did it pass, which protected
terms survived transformation, and what verifiable evidence chain remains?

We formalize a seven-factor behavioral signature Σ(C) = ⟨F₁,…,F₇⟩
comprising Identity DNA, Temporal Anchor, Rule Trace, Persona Route,
Protected Lexicon, Style Vector, and Mistake Ledger. Hard failure in any
factor forces the composite confidence to zero under the stated aggregation.
We state ledger properties (tamper evidence, lineage continuity, correction
preservation, no-silent-laundering) and propositions on the composite score.

We sketch instantiation in the Longhun System, a civilian-grade local-first
environment, arguing that provenance tooling is achievable without institutional
infrastructure. The framework complements GPG, C2PA, and Git; it does not
replace legal judgment or claim mathematical impossibility of forgery.

Keywords: provenance, content authentication, human-AI collaboration,
behavioral signatures, audit trails, digital sovereignty
```
