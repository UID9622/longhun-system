## The Trap Structure

### Layer 1: The Bait

```
Ad: "Learn Python in 30 Days! No Experience Needed! High Salary Guaranteed!"
Reality: 30 days gets you nothing. High salary requires years of practice.
Target: Unemployed, career-switchers, rural youth, people desperate for change.
```

### Layer 2: The Hook

```
Ad: "Free Trial Class! See If Coding Is Right For You!"
Reality: The trial is designed to make you feel "I can do this!" — then the sales pitch hits.
Target: Your hope. Your fear of being left behind.
```

### Layer 3: The Lock-In

```
Ad: "Enroll Now! Limited Spots! Special Discount Ending Soon!"
Reality: High-pressure sales psychology. Artificial scarcity. The "discount" is the real price.
Target: Your FOMO. Your impulse control.
```

### Layer 4: The Debt Trap

```
Ad: "Can't Afford It? We Have Installment Plans! Learn First, Pay Later!"
Reality: You're signing a loan, not an education contract. Interest rates are predatory.
Target: The financially vulnerable. Those without credit literacy.
```

### Layer 5: The Content Void

```
Ad: "Comprehensive Curriculum! Industry Experts! Job Guarantee!"
Reality: Outdated videos, unqualified "instructors," automated grading. No real mentorship.
Target: Those who can't distinguish quality from marketing.
```

### Layer 6: The Blame Shift

```
Ad: "Our Graduates Get Hired! (Thousands of success stories!)"
Reality: When you don't get hired, it's "your fault." You didn't try hard enough. You didn't network enough.
Target: Your self-esteem. So you blame yourself, not the scam.
```

## How to Spot a Coding Education Scam

✅ **Real education:**
- Transparent about time required (realistically: 6-24 months for employability)
- Access to real instructors (not just chatbots or forums)
- No "guaranteed job" promises (they teach, you earn — no one can guarantee)
- Free or low-cost entry (real educators don't need high-pressure sales)
- Verifiable alumni (not just testimonials — actual LinkedIn profiles, GitHub repos)

❌ **Scam indicators:**
- "Learn in weeks" claims
- "Limited time offer" pressure
- "Income sharing agreements" that are actually high-interest loans
- Vague instructor credentials
- No refund policy or impossible refund conditions
- "Success stories" that can't be independently verified

## The Real Numbers

Let's be honest about what coding education actually requires:

```
To become employable as a junior developer:
  - Time: 6-24 months of consistent study (10-20 hours/week)
  - Cost (self-taught): $0-$500 (books, courses, tools)
  - Cost (quality bootcamp): $2,000-$15,000 (legitimate programs)
  - Cost (university degree): Varies by country

What NO legitimate program can promise:
  - A job. Period. Employment depends on you, the market, and timing.
  - "Passive income" from coding. Coding IS the work.
  - That you'll "never need to learn again." Tech evolves continuously.
```

## What To Do If You've Been Scammed

1. **Document everything**: Screenshots, contracts, payment records, promises made in ads.
2. **Contact your bank**: Dispute the charges. Many have fraud protection.
3. **Report to consumer protection**: In China: 12315. Internationally: local consumer affairs.
4. **Speak publicly**: Your story protects others. Scammers thrive on silence.
5. **Don't blame yourself**: You were targeted by professionals whose job is deception. This is not your fault.
6. **Learn anyway**: Being scammed doesn't mean you can't code. Free resources exist: freeCodeCamp, The Odin Project, CS50. Real education is available. No debt required.
