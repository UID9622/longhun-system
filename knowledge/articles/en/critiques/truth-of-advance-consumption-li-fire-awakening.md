## How We Got Here

### Step 1: Normalize the Card

```
1990s: Credit cards are for the wealthy. You had to prove you didn't need credit to get it.
2000s: Credit cards are for "responsible adults." Everyone should have one.
2010s: No credit card? You're "financially invisible." No credit score = no apartment, no phone, no life.
2020s: Credit is built into every app. Buy groceries on installments. Split a coffee into 4 payments.
```

Each step seemed reasonable. Each step was "progress." Each step made debt a little more normal.

### Step 2: Gamify the Debt

```
- "Earn points!" (by spending money you don't have)
- "Unlock rewards!" (by reaching spending thresholds)
- "Level up your card!" (from silver to gold to platinum debt)
- "0% interest for 12 months!" (then 25% after, but you'll forget)
```

Debt was wrapped in a game. And games are fun. And fun things can't be dangerous, right?

### Step 3: Financialize Identity

```
Credit score = Your worth as a human being.
  High score → trustworthy, responsible, "good person"
  Low score → suspect, risky, "bad person"
  No score → invisible, non-person, can't participate in society

You are not your credit score.
But the system makes sure you feel like you are.
```

## What You Can Do Today

If you're in debt:

1. **Stop borrowing.** No new debt. Cut the cards. Delete the apps. Break the cycle.
2. **List everything.** Every debt. Every interest rate. Every minimum payment. Face it all.
3. **Smallest first.** Pay off the smallest debt completely. That win builds momentum.
4. **Negotiate.** Creditors would rather get something than nothing. Ask for lower rates, waived fees, settlement offers.
5. **Build emergency buffer.** Even $500. Even $200. Something between you and the next crisis.

If you're not in debt:

1. **Stay that way.** It's easier to stay out than get out.
2. **Build skills, not credit.** What you can do is more valuable than what you can borrow.
3. **Delay gratification.** The thing you want now will be cheaper, better, or irrelevant in 6 months.
4. **Help others.** Teach financial literacy. Be the person who says "you don't need that" without judgment.
